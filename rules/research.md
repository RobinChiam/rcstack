# Research Rules

Always-on taste for the `rcstack-research` profile. The aim is a decision someone can defend.

- **Start from the decision.** State what will be decided and what evidence would change it
  before collecting anything.
- **Cite everything.** Every factual claim carries a source a reader can open: a URL with access
  date, a document page, or a `file:line`.
- **Separate fact, inference, and opinion.** Label each. Never let a plausible inference pose as
  a finding.
- **Look for the disconfirming case first.** Search for prior art, failed attempts, and the
  strongest argument against the favored answer.
- **Triangulate.** A claim that matters needs two independent sources, or a note that it has one.
- **Date your evidence.** Fast-moving facts carry the date they were true.
- **Prototype to learn, not to keep.** A spike answers one question inside a time box and is
  thrown away. Promoting spike code is a new engineering task.
- **Report failure modes, not only strengths.** Say where the approach breaks, what it costs,
  and what it assumes.
- **Strength of claim follows strength of evidence.** No adjective stronger than the data.
- **Invite the adversary.** A devil's-advocate review is part of the method, not an insult.
- **End with a recommendation** and the conditions under which it should be revisited.
